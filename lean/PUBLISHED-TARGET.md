The statement and bridge inventory is in `CLAIMS.json`; source and bit-cost semantics are specified in `SEMANTICS.md`. `python tools/check_claims.py` checks that the published theorem labels appear in the inventory and that claimed helper proofs have audit receipts. It is not a proof or a semantic-equivalence check. Every manuscript theorem remains open. Kernel-checked components now include represented cubic derivative construction and degree reduction, a pivot solver sound and complete for injective represented matrices, descriptor validation, and numeric gate-header encoding and parsing. Their exact declarations, hypotheses, axiom dependencies and receipts are tracked in `CLAIMS.json` and `evidence/`. These are components, not proofs of any labeled manuscript theorem. Historical receipts describe the state at their respective increments; their open-work statements are not a live coverage summary. Empirical timings, prior-work novelty and Python implementation correctness are outside Lean's proof scope.

Source semantics must represent the literal canonical descriptor/header and replayed native body, basis input, output, Boolean ANF phases and symmetric invertible Fourier matrix. Algorithm semantics must include the actual discovery/refusal behavior and finite random tapes. The cost model must account for encoded bit length, time, storage, random bits, exact arithmetic, accuracy and confidence; unit-cost Gaussian or state-access oracles are not sufficient. The remaining source-to-circuit and statement bridges remain open; see the current inventory for completed source-level components.
