The increment defines the manuscript's basis matrix as an explicit object. `supportBasisMatrix` is an `n`-by-`n` binary matrix whose first `2 * pairCount` columns are the polar-image basis columns `supportBasisColumn` and whose remaining columns are zero, and `extendCoordinates` is the padded coordinate tuple it acts on. Three declarations are recorded. `matrixApply_eq_vectorFoldlXor` proves that applying any binary matrix is the fold of its columns weighted by the argument, which is what lets the matrix be reasoned about column by column. `vectorFoldlXor_apply` reads that fold at one coordinate as the Boolean fold of the readings. `supportBasisMatrix_column` proves that the `j`-th column of the defined matrix is exactly the `j`-th basis column. The first needs no axioms beyond none, the second `propext` and `Quot.sound`, and the third `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`; none depends on `sorryAx`.

An earlier attempt at this increment declared the wrong expected-axiom sets for two of the three declarations; the checker rejected it, which is the check doing its job, and the expectation table was corrected before this receipt. No proof changed.

The scope must be stated precisely, because it is narrower than the coordinate family it packages. What is proved here is the matrix object's column structure and the general column-fold reading of matrix application. What is **not** proved is the action identity: that applying `supportBasisMatrix` to the padded tuple returns `kernelCoordinateElement y`. That identity needs a Boolean fold-distribution lemma, distributing `&&` over the nested column fold, which is not in the development; so the matrix is defined and its columns are identified, but the matrix is not yet shown to compute the coordinate element, and the support is not yet stated as the affine image of the matrix. The computability clause still has no bit-operation cost trace, the matched-sector proposition and the remaining labeled theorems have no coverage, and no published theorem is certified.
