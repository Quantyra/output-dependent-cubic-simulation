One declaration is recorded here. `sourceWalshSum_derivativeANF_eq_differenceTransform` proves that for a degree-at-most-three source phase and any displacement, the computed degree-at-most-two derivative ANF exists, and the formalized source Walsh sum of that ANF equals the signed sum, over the full binary basis, of the difference between the phase at a point and at its shift, taken against the character. It uses `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and does not depend on `sorryAx`.

The point is interpretive. All earlier certified results in this family, the nonempty affine support, its identification as a coset of the source quadratic polar image, the exact magnitude and the sign structure, are statements about `sourceWalshSum` applied to a degree-at-most-two phase. This declaration shows that for the phase the construction actually produces, that sum is the Walsh transform of the first difference of the original phase along the displacement. Those results are therefore about the transform of a difference of the source phase, not about an unrelated abstract object.

This is not the full correspondence with the manuscript's Walsh transform, and this evidence must not be read as supplying it. The manuscript forms its quadratic from the second-layer phase and the specific displacement obtained by inverting the pairing matrix on the output one-hot vector, and its transform is taken with the paper's normalization and sign conventions. What is proved here is the shape of that correspondence for an arbitrary displacement and the formalization's own conventions; tying it to the manuscript's `Q` requires fixing the displacement from the circuit, which the source output-derivative machinery supplies but which is not composed here, and checking the normalization convention. Until that composition exists, the computability clause remains uncertified for the reason recorded at `038f5dc`: its subject is not yet the thing being computed. The magnitude, rank-evenness and support-dimension clauses remain blocked on the rank identification recorded in `lean/SEMANTICS.md`, and the quadratic-sign clause on the support-coordinate bridge. No bit-cost bound is proved for any computed object. The normalized sector identity and denominator, matched-sector boundedness, complete costs and every labeled paper theorem remain uncertified. These are helper results; no published manuscript theorem is certified here, and nothing here establishes quantum advantage or a complexity consequence.
