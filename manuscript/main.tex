\begin{abstract}
We give a constructive method for estimating one output probability of a specified
two-layer Boolean cubic-phase circuit. Differentiating the second phase at the measured
output produces a quadratic Walsh support. Restricting the first cubic tensor to its
displacement space yields a control parameter that can remain constant even when
specified global-control certificates grow. We construct normalized matched sectors
from the source and apply established stabilizer norm estimation to their coherent
sum. For a verified restricted control codimension $k$, the readout uses
$2^k\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta)$ times a fixed polynomial, compared with $4^k$ for
direct coherent branch pairs. General control discovery still requires a full-rank
contraction-anchor promise and costs $2^{O(q^2)}$ at cap $q$; deterministic shortcuts
have separate guarantees. We give exact arithmetic, random-bit and storage bounds,
growing examples, explicit refusals, and a reproducible finite comparison that preserves
the new method's unfavorable small-$k$ constants. This is a source-specific application
and refinement of existing covering-set, independent-space and norm-estimation methods,
not a new general simulation primitive or a universal quantum simulation result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Task, source interface and contribution}\label{sec:source}
Let $V=\F^n$, let $J$ be a symmetric invertible binary matrix, and define
\[
 (F_J)_{x,y}=2^{-n/2}(-1)^{x^TJy},\qquad
 D_f\ket{x}=(-1)^{f(x)}\ket{x}.
\]
Thus $F_J=F_J^*=F_J^{-1}$. The source describes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circuit}
 \ket{\Psi}=F_JD_{f_1}F_JD_{f_0}F_J\ket{\beta},
 \qquad p=\Pr[e^Tx=1\text{ on measuring }\Psi],
\end{equation}
where $f_0,f_1$ have Boolean degree at most three, $\beta$ is a specified basis input,
and $e$ is a specified coordinate vector. All polynomials are reduced algebraic normal
forms: $x_i^2=x_i$, coefficients lie in $\F$, and constant, linear and quadratic terms
are retained. We estimate $p$ within $\epsilon=2^{-b}$ with failure at most $2^{-h}$,
for integers $b,h\geq1$. This is one marginal probability, not full-distribution sampling.

The executable native interface is deliberately literal. A descriptor contains
$n,J,f_0,f_1,\beta,e$ as explicit binary masks. The native gate list begins with an
identity header encoding the descriptor: each ASCII bit is a doubled $X$ or doubled
$Z$ on wire zero, terminated by a doubled $H$. The body is the canonical expansion
of~\eqref{eq:circuit}: reversible linear maps and Hadamards implement $F_J$;
$Z$, controlled-$Z$ and the fixed Clifford+$T$ decomposition of controlled-controlled-$Z$
implement the phase monomials. Constant signs use $XZXZ$. Recognition decodes the header,
validates the descriptor, recompiles it, and compares every body record and boundary
value. Hence the header supplies an encoding of the actual source, not an unchecked
claim of equivalence or a structural certificate. An arbitrary equivalent gate list
need not be recognized. The theorem also applies directly to the explicit descriptor
interface with its actual encoding cost. Let $B$ be the bit length of the chosen literal
source, including its header when present.

The source contains neither a control space nor a stabilizer decomposition. Those are
constructed and verified. The contribution is the resulting original-source theorem:
output-dependent sector construction, a promised discovery procedure, and a joint norm
readout with all access and arithmetic costs included. The conditional quadratic
decompositions are instances of established covering and independent-space methods
\cite{maslov2024,blaser2026}; the linear-length norm estimator is established
\cite{bravyi2019}. Output-dependent marginal access is also prior art
\cite{park2024,nest2011}. Section~\ref{sec:comparison} identifies the interfaces precisely.

\section{Quadratic output reduction and normalized sectors}\label{sec:sectors}
Put $j=J^{-1}e$ and $Q(v)=f_1(v)+f_1(v+j)$. The degree of $Q$ is at most two.
For a quadratic $q$, write
$B_q(u,v)=q(u+v)+q(u)+q(v)+q(0)$ for its alternating polar form and
$W_q(l)=\sum_v(-1)^{q(v)+l^Tv}$ for its Walsh transform.

\begin{lemma}[Quadratic Walsh elimination]\label{lem:walsh}
If $B_q$ has rank $r$, then $r$ is even. The support of $W_q$ is an affine space
$l_0+\im B_q$ of dimension $r$. Every nonzero normalized value $2^{-n}W_q(l)$ has
magnitude $2^{-r/2}$, and its sign is a quadratic function of coordinates on this
support. The support, its origin and this sign can be computed by binary elimination.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a basis in which the polar form is a sum of $r/2$ hyperbolic pairs and has a
radical of dimension $n-r$. In those coordinates $q$ is a sum of pair products plus
linear terms and a constant. Summing a radical coordinate is zero unless its linear
coefficient is canceled by $l$, and otherwise contributes two. Each pair sum is
$\sum_{u,v}(-1)^{uv+au+bv}=2(-1)^{ab}$. Multiplying gives the stated support,
magnitude and quadratic sign, with no enumeration of support points. The same
elimination computes an exact real quadratic Gauss sum in polynomial bit time.
\end{proof}

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:walsh} to $Q$. Write its support as $J(d_0+S)$, where
$S\subset V$ has even dimension $r$. Choose a basis matrix $D:\F^r\to S$ and a
computed quadratic $w$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:walsh}
 2^{-n}W_Q(J(d_0+Dz))=2^{-r/2}(-1)^{w(z)}.
\end{equation}
Let $C:V\to\F^c$, $c=n-r$, be a full-row-rank annihilator of $S$, let $R$ be a
right inverse of $C$, put $\eta=Cd_0$, and solve $Ds=d_0+R\eta$. These are all
computed affine coordinates, including the origin and its matching offset.

The first phase state has amplitudes $2^{-n/2}(-1)^{f_0(x)+(J\beta)^Tx}$.
Its mass on each coset $Cx=a$ is exactly $2^{-c}$. In coordinates on that coset define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi}
 \psi_a(y)=2^{-r/2}(-1)^{g_a(y)},\qquad
 g_a(y)=f_0(Ra+Dy)+(J\beta)^T(Ra+Dy).
\end{equation}
Every $\psi_a$ is a unit vector.

\begin{proposition}[Matched-sector identity]\label{prop:sector}
The pulled-back output observable is the real Hermitian Clifford unitary
\[
 O=F_JD_{f_1}F_J Z_e F_JD_{f_1}F_J=F_JD_QF_JZ_e.
\]
It maps coset $a$ onto coset $a+\eta$. Its reduced kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 O_a(t,y)=2^{-r/2}(-1)^{q_a(y,t)},\quad
 q_a(y,t)=w(y+t+s)+e^T(Ra+Dy).
\end{equation}
The cross bilinear matrix of $q_a$ in $(y,t)$ is nonsingular. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded}
 X_a=\langle\psi_{a+\eta},O_a\psi_a\rangle\in[-1,1],\quad
 X_a=X_{a+\eta},\quad \E_a X_a=1-2p.
\end{equation}
For $u_a=\psi_{a+\eta}+O_a\psi_a$, we have
$\norm{u_a}^2=2+2X_a\in[0,4]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$F_JZ_eF_J$ is translation by $j$. Commuting this translation through $D_{f_1}$
gives $D_Q$, proving the second expression for $O$. Quadratic phase gates and binary
Fourier maps are Clifford. The first expression proves Hermiticity and unitarity.
The matrix entry between $x$ and $x'$ is
$2^{-n}W_Q(J(x+x'))(-1)^{e^Tx}$. Its support condition is $x+x'\in d_0+S$.
Substitute $x=Ra+Dy$, $x'=R(a+\eta)+Dt$ and use~\eqref{eq:walsh} to obtain
\eqref{eq:kernel}. Since $Q(v+j)=Q(v)$, its Walsh support annihilates $j$;
thus $e$ annihilates $S$ and $e^Td_0=0$. Each restricted map is a square unitary
between full cosets. In a flat quadratic kernel, a singular cross matrix would give
two proportional rows, contradicting unitarity. Realness and Cauchy--Schwarz bound
$X_a$; Hermiticity gives its matching symmetry. Expanding the first phase state over
orthogonal cosets of equal mass gives~\eqref{eq:bounded}. Expanding the norm of $u_a$
proves the last assertion. All lower terms and input characters remain in these formulas.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, substitute $x=Ra+Dy$ and displacement $d_0+Dz$ in the full output
polynomial. Its signed numerator $H_a$ has $2r$ free variables and
$X_a=H_a/2^{3r/2}$. This smaller normalization matters: summing terms independently
before retaining their interference would not provide the bounded statistic in
\eqref{eq:bounded}. There is no unknown postselection probability to estimate.

\section{Control certificates and paid discovery}\label{sec:discovery}
The third difference of a Boolean cubic is an alternating trilinear form. Let $T$
be this tensor for $f_0$ restricted to $S$ in the computed $D$ coordinates.
A control certificate is a $k$-dimensional covector space $U\subset(\F^r)^*$ with
$K=\ker U$ and $T(K,K,K)=0$. This is an independent-space condition in the terminology
of~\cite{blaser2026}. It can be checked by tensor evaluation on a basis of $K$.
Translation and addition of lower-degree terms do not change $T$.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional quadratic branches]\label{lem:branches}
A verified control certificate constructs a decomposition of each $\psi_a$ into
$2^k$ normalized stabilizer states, each with coefficient $2^{-k/2}$. Fixing controls
on both sides of $X_a$ yields $4^k$ exact real quadratic sums.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a right inverse of $U$ and a basis of $K$, yielding coordinates $(u,z)$ on
$\F^r$. On each affine slice $u$ the cubic coefficient is $T|_{K^3}=0$, so the full
phase, including its affine terms, is quadratic in $z$. A uniform quadratic phase on
an affine subspace is a normalized stabilizer state. The slice has $2^{r-k}$ points,
which gives coefficient $2^{-k/2}$ in~\eqref{eq:psi}. The kernel in
\eqref{eq:kernel} is quadratic, so fixing both state controls leaves a quadratic
form on $2r-2k$ variables. This is the established covering-set construction in a
computed coordinate frame, not a minimum stabilizer-rank claim.
\end{proof}

We use the following explicit discovery scope, with integer search cap $q\geq0$.
Zero tensor is accepted deterministically. A nonzero one-control factor is detected
by solving $\ell\wedge T=0$ for a covector $\ell$: in a basis with $\ell=e_1^*$,
this equation says every cubic includes that coordinate. Thus a nonempty solution
space supplies a control, and an empty space is a valid cap-one refusal. At larger
caps a basis of the quadratic exterior kernel is scanned for decomposable two-forms;
every proposed image is verified. This is sound but incomplete: a decomposable
linear combination need not be one of the returned basis vectors.

\begin{theorem}[Capped anchor discovery]\label{thm:discovery}
Suppose some certificate has dimension $k\leq q$ and some $v\in K$ has contraction
$A(v)=T(v,\cdot,\cdot)$ of rank $2k$. After deterministic shortcuts and coordinate
probes, inspect at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discoverycost}
 A_q=4^q(h+2)\quad\text{uniform probes},\qquad
 C_q=4(q+1)2^{q^2}\quad\text{candidates per image}.
\end{equation}
Stream all covector subspaces of dimension at most $q$ in each nonzero contraction
image of rank at most $2q$, verifying each. Discovery misses with probability at most
$2^{-h-1}$. Every accepted certificate is sound, even without the promise. Every
random tape obeys the caps; a miss outside the promise has no success guarantee.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $v\in K$, the contraction vanishes on $K\times K$. In coordinates complementary
to $K$ its matrix has block form $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B&M\\M^T&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Rank $2k$ forces $M$ to have row rank $k$, and consequently the contraction image
contains $U$. A principal Pfaffian witnessing rank $2k$ is a degree-$k$ polynomial
in $v\in K$. It is nonzero at the promised anchor. Any nonzero Boolean polynomial
of degree at most $k$ is nonzero on at least a $2^{-k}$ fraction of points: induct on
the degree, using a nonzero highest-variable derivative and one nonzero value in
each corresponding pair. A uniform ambient vector hits $K$ with probability $2^{-k}$.
Thus each probe is useful with probability at least $4^{-k}\geq4^{-q}$.

The number of $d$-subspaces in an $l$-space is a Gaussian binomial coefficient,
at most $4\,2^{d(l-d)}$; indeed its product formula has denominator
$\prod_{i=1}^d(1-2^{-i})>1/4$. For $l\leq2q$ and $0\leq d\leq q$,
$d(l-d)\leq q^2$, proving the stated candidate cap. An image from a useful probe
therefore exposes $U$ to the exhaustive verified search. Independent probe failure is
at most $(1-4^{-q})^{A_q}\leq e^{-(h+2)}\leq2^{-h-1}$.
Coordinate probes and sound shortcuts can only improve success. Rank above $2q$
is skipped, not treated as impossibility. The final complete phase substitution
checks the expected absence of free cubic terms as an additional consistency check.
\end{proof}

A user-imposed candidate limit that truncates this search removes its confidence
guarantee. Small-control existence alone is insufficient for the anchor theorem.
The algorithm returns explicit discovery or grammar refusals; it has no hidden
unbounded fallback and makes no generic gate-equivalence recognition claim.

\section{Joint sector and equatorial norm sampling}\label{sec:norm}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:branches} and the Clifford property, $u_a$ has at most $2^{k+1}$
constructed stabilizer terms. The established norm estimator of
Bravyi et al.~\cite[Section 4.3]{bravyi2019} is therefore applicable with work linear
in that length. We include its elementary moment argument to make our normalization
and joint sampling contract explicit.

Choose independent $d_j\in\mathbb Z_4$ uniformly and independent fair bits $a_{jl}$,
and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equatorial}
 z_A(t)=\sum_j d_jt_j+2\sum_{j<l}a_{jl}t_jt_l\pmod4,\qquad
 \phi_A(t)=2^{-r/2}i^{z_A(t)}.
\end{equation}
This requires exactly $d_r=2r+r(r-1)/2$ fair bits.

\begin{lemma}[Equatorial moments; established norm primitive]\label{lem:moments}
For any complex vector $u$ indexed by $\F^r$, set
$Z=2^r|\langle\phi_A,u\rangle|^2$. Then
\[
 \E Z=\norm{u}^2,\qquad
 \E Z^2=2\norm{u}^4-\sum_t|u(t)|^4,\qquad
 \Var Z\leq\norm{u}^4.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The independent diagonal phases kill every off-diagonal term of the first moment.
For the fourth-order expansion, a surviving tuple $(x,z;y,w)$ must obey
$x_j+z_j=y_j+w_j$ as integers at every coordinate: the difference lies in $[-2,2]$
and must vanish modulo four. Where $x,z$ differ, $(y_j,w_j)$ is either their original
order or its swap. If two differing coordinates have different swap choices, the
associated independent edge phase contributes an odd sign and kills the term.
All choices must therefore agree, leaving exactly the two pairings
$(y,w)=(x,z)$ and $(z,x)$. Their overlap $x=z=y=w$ was counted twice and must be
subtracted. This gives the second-moment identity, including $r=0$ and $u=0$.
\end{proof}

For $u=u_a$, exact access uses
\begin{align}
 A&=\sum_t i^{-z_A(t)}(-1)^{g_{a+\eta}(t)},\nonumber\\
 B'&=\sum_{y,t}i^{-z_A(t)}(-1)^{g_a(y)+q_a(y,t)},\nonumber\\
 Z_{a,A}&=\frac{|2^{r/2}A+B'|^2}{2^{2r}}.\label{eq:exactnorm}
\end{align}
Fixing $k$ controls evaluates $A$ with $2^k$ quadratic $\mathbb Z_4$ sums on $r-k$
variables and $B'$ with $2^k$ such sums on $2r-k$ variables. There is no $4^k$ branch
fallback in this readout. For a quadratic $\mathbb Z_4$ form with even cross
coefficients, let $I$ index its odd linear coefficients and
$L(x)=\sum_{j\in I}x_j\pmod2$. The identity
\[
 \sum_{j\in I}x_j\equiv L(x)+2\sum_{j<l\in I}x_jx_l\pmod4
\]
reduces the sum, up to a constant power of $i$, to
$(G_0+G_1)/2+i(G_0-G_1)/2$, where $G_0,G_1$ are real quadratic sums differing by
$L$. Affine substitution preserves this form. Thus each joint draw uses exactly
$4\cdot2^k$ real Gaussian calls. Because $r$ is even,~\eqref{eq:exactnorm} has an
integer numerator and dyadic denominator. No floating-point cancellation or separate
accuracy budget for a norm oracle is needed.

\begin{theorem}[One accuracy budget]\label{thm:estimator}
Sample $a$ uniformly from matching-orbit representatives and $A$ independently as
in~\eqref{eq:equatorial}, and set $Y=Z_{a,A}/2-1$. Then $\E Y=1-2p$ and
$\Var Y\leq4$. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schedule}
 L=32\,4^b,\qquad K_g=2h+3,\qquad N=LK_g,
\end{equation}
take the median of $K_g$ independent group means of $L$ samples each. Convert it to
a probability, project the final value to $[0,1]$, and round down at $b+2$ fractional
bits. Conditional on sound discovery the returned value differs from $p$ by at most
$3\epsilon/4$, except with probability less than $2^{-h-1}$. On the promise of
Theorem~\ref{thm:discovery}, total discovery/readout failure is less than $2^{-h}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The orbit symmetry in~\eqref{eq:bounded} permits representatives sampled with $c$
bits if $\eta=0$, or $c-1$ bits otherwise (fix a coordinate where $\eta$ is one).
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} gives
$\E[Y\mid a]=X_a$ and $\Var(Y\mid a)\leq(1+X_a)^2$.
For $\mu=\E X_a$, total variance is bounded by
\[
 \E(1+X)^2+\Var X
 =1+2\mu+2\E X^2-\mu^2\leq4-(1-\mu)^2\leq4.
\]
Chebyshev bounds a group's error probability at tolerance $\epsilon$ by $1/8$.
For the median to fail, at least half the independent groups must fail, so a union
bound over subsets gives $2^{K_g}(1/8)^{K_g/2}=2^{-K_g/2}<2^{-h-1}$.
Probability conversion divides the error by two; projection cannot increase it;
rounding adds at most $\epsilon/4$. A union bound adds discovery failure. The
conditional argument is uniform over accepted frames, even those selected randomly
by discovery. Zero norms cause no exception: then $Y=-1$ identically.
\end{proof}

Individual $Y$ values are not bounded by one. Applying bounded-variable Hoeffding to
them or clipping individual samples would be invalid. Only the final probability
is projected. The retained coherent evaluator instead samples the genuinely bounded
$X_a$ with $2(h+2)4^b$ draws and uses $4^k$ Gaussian calls per draw.

\section{Complete resource contract}\label{sec:cost}
Let $m=n+r$, and use $A_q,C_q,N,K_g$ from~\eqref{eq:discoverycost} and
\eqref{eq:schedule}. With elementary dense substitution and binary elimination,
a conservative bound on every random tape, including refusals, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:time}
\begin{split}
 \text{bit time}=\wtO\big(& (B+m+q+2)^{12}
 +(r+A_q)(1+C_q)(m+q+2)^{10}\\
 &+N2^{k+2}(m+q+2)^{10}
 +(N+K_g+1)(m+b+h+q+2)^4\big).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $k$ is the returned dimension; $k\leq q$ supplies an a priori bound before
discovery. The source term pays decoding, native replay, tensor reconstruction,
Walsh support, exterior kernels and all coordinate construction. A cubic has
$O(m^3)$ coefficients and its explicit linear substitutions can be performed within
$O(m^6)$ elementary binary operations; solving the exterior systems and verifying
candidate kernels fits the displayed loose polynomial powers. Each probe uses
binary matrix elimination; each candidate adds tensor and complete phase checks.
The third term pays all affine restrictions, random graph construction and the
real Gaussian calls for the two widths in~\eqref{eq:exactnorm}. These powers are
upper bounds for a straightforward implementation, not fitted performance exponents.

Integers in a quadratic sum and a norm numerator have $O(m)$ bits. Group accumulation
adds $O(\log L)$ bits, and schedules have $O(b+\log(h+2)+q)$ bits. Keeping group
totals while streaming probes, candidates and branches gives the conservative bounds
\begin{align}
 \text{space}&=\wtO\big((B+m+q+2)^8+(m+b+h+q+2)^4\big),\label{eq:space}\\
 \text{fair bits}&\leq rA_q+N\big(c-\mathbf1_{\eta\ne0}+2r+r(r-1)/2\big).\label{eq:bits}
\end{align}
Deterministic shortcuts omit the anchor term. Candidate storage is polynomial because
subspaces are enumerated and checked one at a time. Samples and Gaussian budgets
are checked before any readout draw. Truncation yields a refusal, not a success claim.

If the complete original displacement phase is already quadratic, a retained exact
shortcut uses one real Gaussian sum, no sampling bits and polynomial cost in $b$.
Otherwise sampled evaluation is polynomial in $1/\epsilon$, and exponential in $b$.
On the anchor promise $q^2=O(\log(B+m+2))$ suffices for polynomial total inverse-error
cost. Improving $4^k$ to $2^k$ in readout does not improve the $2^{O(q^2)}$ discovery
term. Neither low restricted rank nor a favorable anchor is asserted for every cubic.

\section{Growing families and scope limits}\label{sec:families}
For even $t\geq2$, take coordinates $c_0,w_0,X\in\F^{t\times t},U,V\in\F^t$,
so $n=t^2+2t+2$, and put
\begin{align}
 f_0&=c_0w_0+\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}U_iV_j,\nonumber\\
 f_1&=c_0w_0+c_0\big(Q_{\det}(X)+U_0V_0\big),\nonumber\\
 Q_{\det}(X)&=\sum_{i,j\text{ even}}
 (X_{ij}X_{i+1,j+1}+X_{i,j+1}X_{i+1,j}).\label{eq:family}
\end{align}
Initially $J=I$, $\beta=0$, and the output is $c_0$.

\begin{proposition}[Restricted versus specified global controls]\label{prop:family}
For~\eqref{eq:family}, $S$ is the span of $X,U_0,V_0$, $r=t^2+2$, and $k=1$ is
detected by the deterministic one-control test. Every global certificate
$T(K_0,K_0,K_0)=0$ of codimension $g$ for the full first cubic satisfies
$g\geq t-\sqrt t$. The output is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:familyprob}
 p=\frac14-2^{-t-1}+2^{-2t-1}<\frac14.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The derivative of $f_1$ is $w_0+Q_{\det}(X)+U_0V_0$. Its polar is nonsingular on
the stated $S$ and zero on its complement, proving the support direction and rank.
Restriction of the first cubic to $S$ leaves $X_{00}U_0V_0$, which has one control.
For the global bound assume $g<t$ (otherwise it is immediate). Intersections of
$K_0$ with the $U$ and $V$ coordinate spaces have dimensions at least $t-g$;
its intersection with the matrix space has codimension at most $g$. Tensor vanishing
forces $u^TXv=0$ on those intersections. Their tensor product has dimension at least
$(t-g)^2$ and lies in the annihilator of $K_0\cap\F^{t\times t}$, of dimension at
most $g$. Hence $(t-g)^2\leq g\leq t$ and $g\geq t-\sqrt t$.

For the probability, Fourier summation over $X$ in the first phase forces the
frequency matrix to equal $UV^T$ for some $U,V$. Thus every nonzero spectral
amplitude has a rank-at-most-one frequency matrix, on which $Q_{\det}$ vanishes.
The $c_0,w_0$ Fourier pair cancels the derivative's $w_0$ sign in the shifted
spectral overlap. The remaining expectation is the squared Fourier-amplitude
average of $(-1)^{l_{U,0}l_{V,0}}$. Expanding
$(-1)^{ab}=(1+(-1)^a+(-1)^b-(-1)^{a+b})/2$ gives four characters.
By Fourier orthogonality each character average equals the first phase state's
autocorrelation under the corresponding physical translation. The zero translation
contributes one. Translation of
$U_0$ alone requires $V=0$ and contributes $2^{-t}$; translation of $V_0$ alone
contributes $2^{-t}$. Translation of both requires $U_i=V_i=0$ for $i>0$ and
$U_0+V_0=1$, contributing $2^{1-2t}$. On every surviving matrix the determinant
blocks vanish. Thus $1-2p=(1+2^{1-t}-2^{1-2t})/2$, which is~\eqref{eq:familyprob}.
\end{proof}

Adding the output linear term to $f_1$ complements $p$ above $3/4$. Arbitrary lower
terms in $f_0$ preserve both tensor certificates. Lower terms in $f_1$ change its
derivative only by an affine term, preserving $S$ but possibly changing $d_0,\eta$.
Basis inputs add linear characters. Invertible physical coordinate changes preserve
the certificate conditions with the Fourier pairing transformed consistently.
These variations remain covered even when~\eqref{eq:familyprob} no longer applies.
One can also add first-phase cubics whose tensor vanishes on $S^3$, preserving small
restricted $k$; the global lower bound is not asserted for every such extension.

The global bound concerns this control certificate, not unrestricted stabilizer rank
or all classical algorithms. In fact the unperturbed formula~\eqref{eq:familyprob}
is cheaper than either sampler. Restricted controls can also grow: for independent
variables $Y_{ij}$ and $d_i$, the cubic $\sum_{i<j}Y_{ij}d_id_j$ requires
$\binom{t-k}{2}\leq k$ for any codimension-$k$ independent space. To see this,
project that space onto $d$ (dimension at least $t-k$) and intersect it with $Y$
(codimension at most $k$). Tensor vanishing places the exterior square of the
projection in an annihilator of dimension at most $k$. Thus $k\geq t-\sqrt{2t}$
is necessary. This delimits the certificate mechanism; it is not a hardness theorem.

\section{Relation to prior simulation interfaces}\label{sec:comparison}
Maslov et al.~\cite[Section II]{maslov2024} condition on covers of cubic variables
to obtain quadratic sums. Lemma~\ref{lem:branches} is that construction after a
computed affine change of coordinates. Bl\"aser et al.~\cite[Definition 1 and
Theorem 41]{blaser2026} use the same independent-space condition and evaluate an
amplitude with a supplied $(r-k)$-space in $O(2^kr^3)$ work. Their Theorem 67 has
recovery work $O((r-k)r^3 2^k)$ and high probability over a random planted model
when the planted dimension exceeds half the ambient dimension by a fixed fraction.
Our slower anchor search gives a per-promised-input guarantee with a tunable
failure budget. These are different quantifiers; we do not improve their planted
recovery bound or claim discovery from small-control existence alone.

Park et al.~\cite[Supplement, Section IV.3, Theorem 4]{park2024} give framed-Wigner
marginal methods for Clifford circuits with specified product inputs and positive
representation access. The first cubic phase state here need not be a product: the
CCZ phase has a flattening minor $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
across one qubit versus the other two. Moving that phase into the operation introduces
a non-Clifford gate. This identifies a missing hypothesis for the literal reduction;
it does not exclude other embeddings with explicitly paid access costs.

The first phase state is computationally tractable in the sense of
Van den Nest~\cite[Sections 4.1 and 4.3]{nest2011}: its amplitudes are evaluable and
its squared magnitudes are uniform. However, $O$ has $2^r$ nonzero entries per row.
The direct efficiently-computable-sparse-operator interface therefore does not yield
a polynomial bound at large $r$. Changing basis transfers the obligation to amplitude
and sampling access for the transformed state.

There is an explicit baseline reduction to the normalized Pauli-average machinery
of Zhang and Zhang~\cite[Section IV.1, Algorithm 1]{zhang2025}. Expand
$D_Q=\sum_l\alpha_l Z_l$ with $\alpha_l=2^{-n}W_Q(l)$, conjugate by $F_J$, and
multiply by $Z_e$. The support invariance proved above ensures the relevant Paulis
commute with $Z_e$ and are Hermitian. There are $2^r$ coefficients of magnitude
$2^{-r/2}$; their absolute sum is $2^{r/2}$. Estimating the normalized signed average
to error $\epsilon/2^{r/2}$ gives a generic $2^r\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta)$ sample
bound. This is an upper bound for that reduction, not a lower bound for their framework.

Finally, Bravyi et al.~\cite[Section 4.3]{bravyi2019} supply the norm primitive and
linear decomposition-length cost. Our integration constructs the needed states and
Clifford access and combines the sector and norm randomness without multiplying two
inverse-error costs. We claim a constructive source-specific theorem, not priority
for its ingredients, an exhaustive non-subsumption result, or best-known performance.

\section{Reproducible finite evidence}\label{sec:evidence}

zero-norm sector, nonzero offsets and inputs, hidden coordinates, and two- and
three-control cases. Eight full native-source runs compare both readouts with
independent exact Fourier-state evolution; small cases additionally replay literal
gates. During the new readout the old $4^k$ branch routine is forbidden by the test.
